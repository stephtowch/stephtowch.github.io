The Burnout Syndrome Test **[(Towch & Pontes, 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11469-025-01603-1)** was developed to provide a brief psychometric assessment for Burnout Syndrome in line with the World Health Organization's classification in the ICD-11 **[(WHO, 2025)](https://icd.who.int/browse/2025-01/mms/en#129180281)** that includes 1) feelings of energy depletion or exhaustion; 2) increased mental distance from one’s job, or feelings of negativism or cynicism related to one's job; and 3) a sense of ineffectiveness and lack of accomplishment. The BST was developed to provide a more current assessment of Burnout Syndrome as most existing assessment tools were created before this definition and do not align with it. 

At the psychometric level, the BST has been found to be reliable and to offer a unidimensional factor structure for the measurement of Burnout via 9 items that capture the key symptoms of Burnout Syndrome as reported in the literature. The BST was originally developed in two samples: a non-representative employee group of 245 individuals and a nationally representative sample of 758 employed individuals.

The BST has been shown to exhibit adequate psychometric validity in the initial study assessing its factorial validity via Confirmatory Factor Analysis **[(see Towch & Pontes, 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11469-025-01603-1)**

The BST is freely available to any interested individual and is particularly suitable to both time- and resource-limited research on burnout syndrome behaviour. Future studies using clinical samples, longitudinal, and cross-cultural designs are necessary to further consolidate the initial psychometric validation findings reported in the original study.
